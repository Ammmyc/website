Background & Mission

Why this project exists.

Ethnic minority elderly women in Hong Kong often live with quiet hardship—language barriers, social isolation, and cultural stigmas around mental health. CLRI was designed to meet them where they are: through their own stories.

The problem we're addressing

Elderly women from Nepali, Indian, Thai, Pakistani, and Filipino backgrounds in Hong Kong face overlapping disadvantages: limited access to mental health services, language barriers in clinical settings, and cultural reluctance to seek help framed as "treatment for problems."

Conventional therapy models are often a poor fit. Something else was needed—something that begins with strength, not deficit; with story, not symptom.

Our mission & objectives

To improve mental health among ethnic minority elderly through a culturally appropriate, story-based intervention rooted in compassion-focused therapy and life-review practice.

  • Recruit and engage 100+ EM elderly women across 5 communities
  • Co-construct individual life-story booklets with each participant
  • Cultivate self-compassion, self-efficacy, and meaning-making
  • Reduce loneliness, depressive symptoms, and self-criticism
  • Build a foundation for Phase 2 & 3 community expansion
Project Impact

Evidence-based outcomes.

Pre- and post-intervention measurements across 101 participants showed statistically significant improvements in mental health and social wellbeing (p < 0.05).

The Method

What is CLRI?

CLRI brings together two evidence-based approaches: Compassion-Focused Therapy and Life-Review Intervention. Together, they help elderly recognise the meaning and strength in their own life journey.

A CLRI workshop in progress — participants illustrating significant life moments together.
Compassion-based · Life Review Intervention

With interviewers' guidance, participants co-construct life stories that empower them.

Life balance

Counteracts negative biases with balanced assessments of memories and reflection on life.

Memory elaboration

Counters overgeneralised memory as a psychopathological feature.

Self-compassion

Links problems to the common human predicament and adopts a wider perspective.

Resources discovery

Elicits positive emotions, strengths, and meanings during reminiscence.
